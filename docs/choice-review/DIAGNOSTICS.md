## Setup and comparison limits

Three birth-seed profiles started from identical physical conditions: cooking/gardening PIP, making/cooking BRASS and gardening/making FERN. The same need values, stock, traits, empty plots, undamaged home and three-item first delivery were used. Names and seeds identify synthetic diagnostic roosters; they are not wallets or token IDs. The [fixture record](proofs/diagnostic-fixtures.json) gives the complete shared basis and actual seeded preferences.

The old-policy baseline ran one day per profile. R1 and R2 each ran that matched day, then used each actual result as the next day's physical basis with its actual bounded executed-action history. Exact equality between each day-three basis and preceding final state was verified. Incidents and black swans were disabled to isolate ordinary choice behavior. R3 was a predeclared, maker-only follow-up to one observed meal-coherence issue, not another three-profile test.

State, available materials, home affordances, daily inclination and previous-day memory evolve together between days. Consequently, a changed next-day project does not by itself prove that memory caused the change. The baseline already had three different projects across the three matched profiles. None of these diagnostics guarantees that each NPC or every day will choose a unique routine.

Previous-day context contains up to two bounded sets of executed non-T01 records, not a complete biography or every ordinary choice. Current-day executed meaningful records include genuine ordinary choices; local quiet time does not masquerade as an additional Jev decision. Seed mappings remain stable. Actual provider selections are retained; probability telemetry is not manually resampled or treated as a calibrated guarantee.

## What improved, and what did not

The following comparison uses only the three matched day-two cases, keeping its denominator consistent:

| Observation | Baseline | R1 | R2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Distinct main projects | 3 of 3 | 3 of 3 | 3 of 3 |
| Morning coffee immediately followed by another coffee at the first routine beat | 3 of 3 | 3 of 3 | 0 of 3 |
| Near-full-energy nap at the bedtime routine beat | 2 of 3 | 2 of 3 | 0 of 3 |

“Near full” here is a reporting category of at least 90% energy, not a new rule banning rest. The full R1 set contained five near-full late naps among six days. R2 had three late naps among six days: useful 12-point rests beginning at 73% and 71% energy, plus one free five-point nap at 95% for the unhurried cooking profile on day three. The latter is a low-value habit, not a demonstrated safety defect or a reason to force every NPC into a different action.

The matched main projects remained rose planting, a comfort bowl and chair building under all three policies. This sample therefore does not show an increase in the count of different main projects between NPCs. In both R1 and R2, each of the three evolved next-day projects differed from its own preceding project: roses → bowl, bowl → chair and chair → roses. Some alternatives naturally followed from changed stock and the existing chair; memory's independent contribution was not isolated.

Morning styles also remain soft preferences. R1's simple-style gardening profile chose quick coffee while the other two chose rituals; R2's three matched mornings all chose rituals. The R1 quick-coffee example must not be used as proof of final-policy morning-style differentiation. What R2 did demonstrate was that all three selected a different kind of first routine after that habit, rather than automatically preparing a second coffee.

Genuine new-home follow-ups occurred: planting was followed by watering the actual plot, and a newly built chair was followed by enjoying that chair. These came from offered legal actions and real Jev choices. The cooking profile also chose the same garden view three times while anticipating its planting project. That can read as a coherent ongoing gardening afternoon; literal recurrence alone is not proof of aimless movement.

## R3: the remaining toast-before-bowl choice

R2's maker selected toast at slot 41 before its planned comfort bowl at 46. R3 supplied the actual project action/materials, five remaining slots, nominal successful recovery of 50 food/20 water/20 morale, and an explicit warning that success was not guaranteed. The [focused meal-context witness](proofs/r3-meal-context.json) preserves those facts, the actual request/response telemetry and the resulting actions.

**The extra context did not change that sequence.** Jev still made toast at 41, raising food 90→100, then made the bowl at 46. The bowl added no food, but genuinely added 7 water and 10.76 morale while spending its listed milk and bread. The late nap began at 73% energy and recovered 12 points. No result or probability was changed to make this follow-up look successful.

The context addition improves factual information available to the model; these data do not establish that it optimizes future meal recovery. The residual choice is recorded for later balance work rather than removed through arbitrary forced novelty. No further resampling was used to obtain a preferred answer.
